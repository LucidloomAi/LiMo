The guardian’s screens

What a guardian sees in LiMo, in the Paper and Ink theme: sign in, the first start and Today, then Rules, Devices, Add a phone, a one time code and More. A guardian opens these screens in a window on the family PC, and later the same screens run on a guardian’s phone. They are one set of screens arranged by the width they get, never two designs: every frame is drawn twice, side by side, at its real size in CSS pixels. The window is 1100 by 760 and its top 32 is Windows’ own title bar. The phone is 390 by 844, with Android’s status bar in its top 32 and its gesture bar in its bottom 24.

C0. Sign in

LiMo’s settings open with the guardian password. A message under the field has its room kept, so nothing moves when one appears. What to do about a forgotten password is said on the screen itself, because there is nothing to tap for it. From a child’s Windows account the screen says it cannot be opened, and has no field.

F1 to F8. The first start: making the family

One step a screen, with Back and a counter above the card, and the main button pinned to the bottom. On the first step, Back’s room is kept empty so the counter never moves. The sentence about the window on F1 is said only in the console on the PC, where Windows draws the window, so a phone leaves it out. Blocked programs and apps, and how much of the day a phone away from home may use, are not part of the first start: they live in Rules.

Unrolled: the frames that scroll

Each frame that does not fit its window, drawn at its full length. In use it scrolls above the pinned button.

T. Today, the home screen

What is happening now, and the answers a guardian gives most, each one tap. At the console’s width Today uses two columns: the children at the left, and what waits for a guardian at the right. At 390 it is one column, and what waits comes first. Marigold is time, teal is in use or done, coral is locked or needs a guardian. The tab bar floats in glass at the bottom centre at both widths, and the page keeps room at its end so the last row scrolls clear of it.

Unrolled: the frames that scroll

Each frame that does not fit its window, drawn at its full length, with the tab bar at the end where it rests when the page is scrolled to the bottom.

R. A child’s rules

Everything a family sets for one child, the child chosen at the top. On a phone the cards are one column. At the console’s width they run down two columns in the same order. Programs on the PC are offered from what the child really used; apps on a phone are listed by the names the phone gives them. When something is changed, the tab bar gives way to Undo and Save, pinned to the bottom. Save checks the rules first, and a problem is said in the rules’ own sentence beside the row it is about. Saved, a notice floats above the tab bar for a moment.

D. Devices

What each device is and how it stands. At the console’s width this PC is at the left and the phones at the right; on a phone the phones come first, because a phone is what may need a guardian. Coral marks what does: a step switched off on the phone, USB debugging left on, a clock far off.

A1 to A9. Add a phone

One step a screen, as in the first start, with the cable’s state live at the foot of every step: read once a second, beside the main button on the PC and above it on a phone. A step that waits for the phone keeps its button switched off and moves on by itself. The phone asks once to allow this PC’s key, and the screen shows the key so a guardian can compare it. Installing asks nothing on the phone. The phone’s own six steps are done on the phone while this screen shows which are on. At the end LiMo offers to switch USB debugging off again.

The cable’s state, in each case

Only the pinned strip. The PC tells these apart: no phone on any USB port, so the cable probably only charges; a phone with USB debugging off; a phone whose debugging side has no working driver on Windows; a phone that has not allowed this PC yet; and connected.

K. A one time code

For a child whose device is out of reach or locked, read out over a call. Choose the child, the device and how much; the code is shown large, in two groups of four. Codes work on the day they were made, ten a day for each device.

M1 to M4. More

A one time code, the password and the PIN, and About. At the console’s width the password and the PIN stand side by side, as in the first start. About ends with the maker’s mark. Deleting the family, removing a child, removing a phone and removing LiMo each ask for the password again; deleting the family is drawn as the pattern for all four.

W1 to W3. Guardians, by name

The family’s guardians become named people, as its children are. They still share one password and one PIN, so nothing about signing in changes: the window cannot tell which guardian typed the password, and it never claims to. A name says who did something only where that is known, from a guardian’s own phone. The first start asks the first guardian’s name after the password, and a family made before is asked once, after sign in. More gains Guardians.

W4 to W8. Add a guardian’s phone

Over the cable, as a child’s phone is added, in Add a phone’s nine steps, so a guardian who has added a child’s phone knows it already. The family’s PC puts LiMo Guardian on the phone and pairs it over the cable, and the cable’s state is at the foot of every step. Only the steps that differ from Add a phone are drawn here: the start, putting LiMo Guardian on the phone, the phone’s own part, and Done, which says plainly what the phone can do and what stays on the PC.

W9 to W12. Where a guardian’s phone shows

Devices lists a guardian’s phone with its guardian, what it is, when it was last seen and the codes it keeps, and removes it with the password again. Today says who gave time or answered a request when it was done from a guardian’s phone. Add a phone, which is for a child’s phone, says where a guardian’s own phone is added. A one time code says how many codes are left for the device today, here and kept for a guardian’s phone.
