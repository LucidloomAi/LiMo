LiMo · design · the guardian’s console

The guardian’s screens: sign in, the first start and Today

What a guardian sees in LiMo, in the Paper and Ink theme. A guardian opens these screens in a window on the family PC, and later the same screens run on a guardian’s phone. They are one set of screens arranged by the width they get, never two designs: every frame is drawn twice, side by side, at its real size in CSS pixels. The window is 1100 by 760 and its top 32 is Windows’ own title bar. The phone is 390 by 844, with Android’s status bar in its top 32 and its gesture bar in its bottom 24.

C0. Sign in

LiMo’s settings open with the guardian password. A message under the field has its room kept, so nothing moves when one appears. What to do about a forgotten password is said on the screen itself, because there is nothing to tap for it. From a child’s Windows account the screen says it cannot be opened, and has no field.

F1 to F8. The first start: making the family

One step a screen, with Back and a counter above the card, and the main button pinned to the bottom. On the first step, Back’s room is kept empty so the counter never moves. The sentence about the window on F1 is said only in the console on the PC, where Windows draws the window, so a phone leaves it out. Blocked programs and apps, and how much of the day a phone away from home may use, are not part of the first start: they live in Rules.

Unrolled: the frames that scroll

Each frame that does not fit its window, drawn at its full length. In use it scrolls above the pinned button.

T. Today, the home screen

What is happening now, and the answers a guardian gives most, each one tap. At the console’s width Today uses two columns: the children at the left, and what waits for a guardian at the right. At 390 it is one column, and what waits comes first. Marigold is time, teal is in use or done, coral is locked or needs a guardian. The tab bar floats in glass at the bottom centre at both widths, and the page keeps room at its end so the last row scrolls clear of it.

Unrolled: the frames that scroll

Each frame that does not fit its window, drawn at its full length, with the tab bar at the end where it rests when the page is scrolled to the bottom.
