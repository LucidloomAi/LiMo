Setting up a child’s phone

A guardian holds the child’s phone. The family PC has just installed LiMo on it over the USB cable and paired it, so the phone already knows the child’s name. The guardian goes through the setup, then hands the phone to the child for a short introduction. Each phone is drawn at its real size in CSS pixels, with Android’s status bar at the top and its gesture bar at the bottom, and the main button pinned to the bottom as on the ways out.

Before setup

Only when LiMo is opened on a phone the PC has not paired. Nothing on the phone can set it up alone. LiMo’s name stays at the top, and the card stands in the middle of the screen.

Welcome, and what LiMo does

The guardian learns what LiMo does on this phone, and agrees, before Android is asked for anything. Continue stays switched off until the box is ticked.

Six permissions, one at a time

One layout for all six: why LiMo needs it, what Android will show, and where it stands. Each is drawn not yet on; Usage access is drawn a second time, done, and Accessibility a second time, after Android’s screen was opened twice and it is still off. A step Android will not turn on never traps a guardian: setup goes on, and Today’s banner names the step.

Done

With a step still off the phone works, so the screen still says it is set up. That step’s row has a ring in place of its tick and NOT ON at its end, and one sentence says where a guardian can turn it on.
