The lock, the block screen and Today on the phone

What a child sees on an Android phone, in the Paper and Ink theme. These are drawings to agree the look, each at its real size in CSS pixels. The words are the PC lock screen’s own, with “PC” changed to “phone” and “programs” to “apps”. The top 32 of every phone is Android’s status bar and the bottom 24 its gesture bar.

A. The lock: time is used up

The main state. The reason first, then what can be done, then the apps that stay open. Emergency call is pinned to the bottom of every lock and block screen, so it is always in sight: a lock never stands between a child and an emergency call. A call is never a way around the limits, and never trapped behind a lock: a ringing call is never covered, and while a call is on the phone app itself stays free, so a call can always be answered, used and ended. During a call the lock carries a way back to it, in teal, pinned above Emergency call.

B. The ways out

On a phone a way out takes the screen instead of sitting beside the reason. The reason stays in sight as a strip at the top of the card, and the main button is pinned to the bottom, above the keyboard when it is up. Emergency call comes at the end of the page.

A wrong code, the wait after too many wrong tries, and a request that waits. Only the card is drawn.

C. The lock: bedtime

As A, with the moon. Only a guardian’s PIN opens it.

D. The block screen

Shown when a blocked app comes to the front. During a call, the same teal Back to the call button sits above Emergency call, as on the lock in A2.

E. Today, the child’s own screen in the app

The time left, the hours when screens are off, and the minutes each app took today: names and minutes only. Looking at LiMo’s own screen never uses time, so nothing is counted while a child reads it, and it says so.

F and G. Over the child’s own app

A warning floats over whatever the child has open, so it carries no maker’s mark. Android draws its own notifications, so only their words are LiMo’s.
